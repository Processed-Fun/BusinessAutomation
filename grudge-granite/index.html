What we do

Most headstones say "beloved" and leave it at that. Ours say what you actually spent forty years thinking about.

Hand-carved grievances

Three generations of stonemasons cut every letter by hand. Your complaint about the ladder deserves nothing less.

Six granites, one message

Charcoal, black, rose, gray, green, and mahogany. Pick the stone that matches the mood of the original incident.

Annual graveside readings

Our Eternal Reading Plan sends a licensed Grievance Officer to read your grudges aloud at the grave once a year, so nobody gets comfortable.

How it works

You do the resenting. We handle the paperwork and the chisel.

  1. Pre-register your grievances

    Sit down with one of our archivists and get it all on record: the ladder, the thermostat, the thing your brother-in-law did. He knows what he did.

  2. Annual review

    Grudges age. Some deepen, some get replaced by better ones. We interview you every year to keep your file current.

  3. Choose your stone and lettering

    Work with our design team on granite color, typeface, ornaments, and whether the neighbor's full legal name fits on one line.

  4. We set it in stone

    When the time comes, we carve your file exactly as registered. We don't soften the wording, we don't take edits from the family, and we certainly don't "let it go." Letting go is what the competition sells.

The family behind the feuds

Grudge Granite was founded in 1961 by Aldo Passero, who wanted his own headstone to mention a specific card game. His grandchildren run the shop today and still won't say who won.

Three generations deep

The Passeros have been carving Vermont granite for over sixty years. Each generation inherits the tools, the shop, and several open files.

Our most famous stone

The original "HE NEVER RETURNED MY LADDER" stone, placed in 1998, still gets visitors. Gary maintains his innocence. See the gallery for the full story.

Built for volume

Some clients need the back of the stone. Some need an appendix. One client in Rutland needed a bench. We don't judge, we measure.
