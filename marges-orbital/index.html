How It Works

Counter to Orbit in Four Steps

Same routine since 1994, give or take a scrubbed Tuesday.

1

Take a booth

Sit wherever, unless you booked a launch. Launch customers get booth six. It rattles the least, and the window faces the pad.

2

Order

Marge takes your eggs and your orbital parameters on the same green pad. If you don't know your inclination, she'll pick one. She's good at it.

3

We bake and fuel

Bill fuels Sweet Marge II behind the silo while your pie warms in the case. Neither of those gets rushed. Ask Bill why once, and only once.

4

Liftoff

Launches go weather and church permitting. Your satellite goes up, your check comes out, and the regulars clap at the window like they didn't clap last week.

The Boss

Marge Does the Math

Marge ran the numbers for the co-op for twenty years before she bought the diner, and she never stopped running numbers. She works out your insertion burn on the same pad she takes your order on, usually between refills, usually faster than the software Bill bought her one Christmas.

She has scrubbed exactly four launches for weather and one for a pie emergency. She stands by all five calls.

Pad Operations

Bill Runs the Back Lot

Mission control is a feed-store counter in the barn with two monitors older than most of our customers. Bill built the pad himself in '94, pours the propellant like it's syrup, and keeps the big red button under a cover marked ASK MARGE FIRST.

He has never once pushed it without asking Marge first. That rule survived two anniversaries and one tornado warning.

Best Seat in the House

Booth Six

Every launch customer gets booth six for the duration of their campaign. It has the clearest view of the pad, a plaque, and the table that rattles the least at ignition. Regulars know to clear out on launch days. Most of them stand in the parking lot anyway, holding their coffee.

If the booth is taken when you arrive, someone lied about having a launch. It happens more than you'd think.
