# Archetypes and Styles

Controlled vocabularies for the Archetype and Style columns in `ideas_index.md`. Always use a value from these lists, spelled exactly as written. If nothing on a list genuinely fits, invent a new value and add it to the list in the same commit, so the vocabulary stays controlled.

Keeping these as fixed values (not free-text descriptions) is what makes variety checkable. Ten near-identical sites should get the same label ten times, not ten flattering synonyms.

# Archetypes

The archetype is the *kind of website* the business would have: its structure, pages, and how you buy. It is part of the business's identity and is assigned when the idea is generated (see `generate_ideas.md`). The examples in parentheses are flavor, not sub-labels; the column value is just the bold slug.

1. **landing-page**. A single long-scroll marketing page: hero, product sections, pricing, footer. Other pages are secondary.
2. **ecommerce-catalog**. A multi-page store: browsable product grid, product detail pages, cart or checkout flow.
3. **single-product**. One product, one obsessive page devoted entirely to it.
4. **saas-product**. A startup or software product site: feature sections, tiered pricing, logo bar, signup or waitlist. (Seed-stage stealth page, dev tool with docs, mobile app page.)
5. **local-brochure**. A small-business brochure site: hours, services, about us, directions, contact. (Restaurant with menu, contractor, salon.)
6. **professional-services**. A firm: services, team bios with credentials, consultation form. (Law firm, clinic, consultancy, funeral home.)
7. **corporate**. A faceless megacorp: divisions, investor relations, values and ESG pages, press releases.
8. **gov-institutional**. An agency or public utility: forms, notices, dense navigation, PDFs. (DMV-style portal, municipal site.)
9. **nonprofit**. A charity or foundation: donation flow, impact stats, campaigns, giving tiers.
10. **editorial**. Articles are the primary content: newspaper, magazine, blog, review site.
11. **infomercial**. Direct-response selling: one relentless page, countdowns, testimonial blocks, ORDER NOW. (As-Seen-On-TV, supplement site, MLM opportunity.)
12. **event**. A conference, festival, or venue: agenda, speakers or lineup, tickets, sponsors.
13. **education**. An academy, course catalog, bootcamp, or certification body.
14. **listings**. Inventory to browse that isn't a store: real estate, marketplace, aggregator, comparison site.
15. **hospitality-venue**. A hotel, resort, or event venue: room and space galleries, amenities, rates, booking inquiry. (Bed and breakfast, wedding venue, campground.)
16. **tour-operator**. A travel or tour company: destination photography, itineraries, dates and prices, booking. (Adventure outfitter, walking tours, expedition cruises.)
17. **cpg-brand**. A packaged food or drink brand: product beauty shots, flavor lineup, recipes, store locator.
18. **studio-portfolio**. A studio or agency that sells through a gallery of its work: projects, process, client list, inquiry form. (Photography studio, design shop, taxidermist.)

# Styles

The style is the visual aesthetic: typography, palette, density, era, mood. It is chosen when the website is built (see `website_creation.md`), because it must be checked against recently built sites.

1. **luxury-minimal**. Vast whitespace, thin serifs or geometric sans, muted palette, few words.
2. **saas-gradient**. Contemporary startup polish: soft gradients, rounded cards, friendly bold sans.
3. **startup-modern**. Clean modern sans, high contrast, product-company feel without the gradients.
4. **elegant-serif**. Warm, tasteful serif typography, cream or parchment tones, artisanal or heritage mood.
5. **web-1990s**. GeoCities era: tiled backgrounds, visitor counters, default-looking fonts, seams showing.
6. **corporate-2000s**. Early-2000s corporate: gradients, bevels, clip art, drop shadows on everything.
7. **brutalist**. Raw, anti-design: system fonts, harsh borders, unstyled-on-purpose.
8. **craft-organic**. Kraft paper, hand-drawn touches, earthy palette, farmers-market energy.
9. **government-austere**. Dense, plain, form-heavy. Looks like it was designed by a committee in a basement.
10. **broadsheet**. Newspaper: columns, rules, small caps, restrained black and white.
11. **y2k-chrome**. Chrome text, lens flares, techno-futurism as imagined in 2001.
12. **vaporwave**. Neon pink and teal, retro-future, glow.
13. **infomercial-max**. Starbursts, red and yellow, ALL CAPS, urgency everywhere.
14. **dark-luxury**. Black and gold, moody photography, exclusivity.
15. **funeral-serenity**. Soft blues and grays, script accents, gentle and hushed.
16. **terminal**. Monospace, green or amber on black, hacker austerity.
17. **flat-pastel**. Scandinavian flat design, pastels, geometric simplicity.
18. **americana-roadside**. Diner signage, slab display type, hand-painted small-town warmth.
19. **academic**. Looks typeset: justified text, footnotes, restraint, LaTeX energy.
20. **sports-neon**. High-energy athletic branding: neon accents, heavy display sans, action angles.

# Choosing a combination

1. Default to playing it straight: pick the archetype and style this business's fictional webmaster would honestly pick. The humor comes from executing a real genre earnestly around an absurd premise.
2. A deliberate mismatch is allowed only with an in-world alibi. Litmus test: write one in-world sentence explaining why the site looks this way ("Hank Jr. made it in FrontPage in 2003 and nobody has touched it since"). If you can't, pick again.
3. The style must serve the site's one joke, never add a second unrelated joke.
