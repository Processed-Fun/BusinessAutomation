Our story

Thirty years of probability was enough

Werner Alby taught statistics at a small Vermont college for three decades. He spent his career explaining to teenagers that the previous roll tells you nothing about the next one. Nobody ever liked hearing it, and toward the end, neither did he.

So he retired to his barn, bought a lathe, and started making dice that keep their promises. Each one is shaped from a single blank, hand polished for forty minutes, and stamped with its number on all six faces. His daughter Ruth runs the shop now. Werner still checks every die before it ships, a process he describes as "brief."

"People say the fun of dice is not knowing what comes next. Those people have never rolled a six on demand in front of their brother-in-law." Werner Alby, founder
