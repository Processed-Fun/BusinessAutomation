# Business Ideas

Fake business ideas that do not yet have websites. Append new ideas to the end.

When a website is built for an idea, its entry is copied to `business.md` in the site's directory and removed from this file, and its row in `ideas_index.md` is updated. The full historical list of every idea, built or not, lives in `ideas_index.md`.

## The Parting Shot

**Short description:** A high-end photography studio specializing in divorce shoots.

**Long description:** The Parting Shot brings the full wedding-photography treatment to the other end of the arc: editorial coverage of the signing day, "first look at the paperwork" sessions, tasteful courthouse-steps portraiture, and commemorative albums with foil-stamped covers. Package tiers range from a single "Decree in Golden Hour" session to full-day coverage including both attorneys. The portfolio is presented with the hushed, romantic tone of a fine-art wedding studio, applied without comment to its subject.

**Themes and joke:** Dark humor played straight (theme 3). The joke is wedding photography's entire visual and verbal genre — the reverence, the storytelling, the albums — aimed at the opposite occasion.

**Archetype:** studio-portfolio


## Twig & Perch Realty

**Short description:** A luxury real-estate brokerage for birdhouses.

**Long description:** Twig & Perch Realty is a full-service brokerage whose MLS-style listings are all birdhouses. Each listing has hero photography, square inchage, perch count, sun exposure, and frank disclosure notes ("HOA has an ongoing matter with the neighborhood cat"). Filters cover architectural style from rustic gourd to midcentury modern, and every agent bio ends with a commitment to finding the right fit "for you and your flock." Open house showings are held at dawn.

**Themes and joke:** A large industry played at tiny scale, with a dash of luxury-where-no-market-exists (themes 8 and 4). The joke is the complete machinery of a real-estate site — listings, filters, agents, disclosures — operating at birdhouse scale.

**Archetype:** listings


## Miller & Sons Weather Modification

**Short description:** A family-owned cloud-seeding contractor with the website of a small-town HVAC company.

**Long description:** Miller & Sons has been "modifying weather since 1962," and their website is pure local trade contractor: fleet photos of trucks with the family crest, three generations of Millers pointing at the sky, and a service menu covering wedding-day clearing, drought relief, ski-season top-ups, and frost protection for orchards. Before-and-after sky photos, a service-area map, financing options, seasonal coupons ("10% off spring rain, book by March"), and a 24-hour emergency line for urgent atmospheric needs. Licensed, bonded, and insured, it says, without specifying by whom.

**Themes and joke:** Planet-scale geoengineering played as a mom-and-pop trade business (theme 8), with a wink at dubious legality (theme 6). The joke is atmospheric physics sold with the warmth and legal confidence of a driveway-paving outfit.

**Archetype:** local-brochure


## The Standing Invitational

**Short description:** The premier championship of competitive waiting in line, presented like the Masters.

**Long description:** The Standing Invitational covers the sport of waiting with full broadcast solemnity: athlete profiles with career statistics (total hours held, posture ratings, personal-best queue lengths), qualifying heats at storied venues like the Midtown DMV and a Saturday deli counter, and a six-hour freestanding final. The site has slow-motion sports photography of people standing still, a records book, a wall of sponsors, hospitality packages, tiered tickets, and merchandise. Past champions receive the ceremonial Velvet Rope.

**Themes and joke:** An incredibly mundane act elevated with full prestige-sport ceremony (theme 7). The joke is Masters-grade broadcast gravity — stats, heritage, hushed commentary — around the least athletic act there is.

**Archetype:** event


## Monogrape

**Short description:** A venture-backed last-mile delivery platform that delivers exactly one grape.

**Long description:** Monogrape is a logistics startup whose entire product is the on-demand delivery of a single grape. The site has app screenshots with live grape tracking, photography of couriers in branded gear wearing tiny insulated single-grape backpacks, and a signature unboxing experience involving a great deal of tissue paper. Surge pricing applies during brunch. There is an enterprise tier for weddings and conferences, a developer API, customer case studies, and a sustainability page pledging grape-neutral logistics by 2030.

**Themes and joke:** A useless product run through the full hot-startup ceremony (themes 5 and 7). The joke is a complete DoorDash-grade logistics network, funding story and all, pointed at one grape.

**Archetype:** saas-product


## The Pothole Preservation Society

**Short description:** A heritage nonprofit fighting to preserve historic potholes.

**Long description:** The Society maintains an endangered-pothole registry with formal portrait photography, dedicates bronze plaques beside particularly storied craters, and leads guided walking tours of a town's most significant depressions. Restoration projects are documented with before-and-after photos (they add water). Giving tiers run from Gravel Friend to the Adopt-a-Pothole program, where donors receive a certificate and quarterly condition updates on their pothole. The year ends with a black-tie gala held, weather permitting, outdoors near the potholes.

**Themes and joke:** The full historic-preservation apparatus — urgency, plaques, galas, adoption programs — deployed to defend the one piece of heritage everyone wants gone.

**Archetype:** nonprofit


## DullMaster Pro

**Short description:** An As-Seen-On-TV knife so safe it cannot cut anything.

**Long description:** DullMaster Pro is sold with every direct-response beat: slow-motion demo footage of the blade failing against a ripe tomato, a jubilant family passing it to a toddler, a comparison chart against "dangerous" knives, and testimonials from nine-fingered converts who wish they'd switched sooner. The countdown timer resets when it hits zero, and the inevitable BUT WAIT doubles your order into the twelve-piece DullMaster Family Block, with knives equally incapable of cutting bread, steak, or anything else.

**Themes and joke:** A useless product (theme 5) in full infomercial regalia. The joke is infomercial urgency celebrating a knife for failing at the one thing a knife must do.

**Archetype:** infomercial


## Demands by Design

**Short description:** A boutique design studio for ransom notes.

**Long description:** Demands by Design presents its ransom notes the way a prestige agency presents brand work: a portfolio of framed cut-letter compositions with case-study write-ups ("the client needed urgency without hysteria"), a process page on ethically sourcing magazines, and typography studies on which serifs read most menacing. Client testimonials are enthusiastic and entirely anonymous. There is an awards shelf, a careers page seeking juniors with strong scissor skills, and an inquiry form that promises absolute discretion.

**Themes and joke:** Dubious legality (theme 6) filtered through prestige design-agency portfolio culture. The joke is the full agency ceremony — process, craft, awards — applied to a genre with exactly one client demand.

**Archetype:** studio-portfolio
