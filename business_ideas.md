# Business Ideas

A running list of verified fake business ideas. Append new ideas to the end — never delete existing ones.

---

## 1. Full Bird Hydration

**Short description:** A sports drink company whose entire flavor lineup is Thanksgiving dinner.

**Long description:** Full Bird Hydration makes electrolyte drinks for "serious athletes with serious appetites," offering flavors like Turkey & Gravy Surge, Cranberry Stuffing Splash, Green Bean Casserole Chill, and a limited holiday release called Giblet Blast. Every bottle promises "22 grams of electrolytes and the unmistakable essence of a food coma." The company sponsors competitive eaters and one very confused marathon runner.

**Themes and joke:** Gross flavors (theme 1). The joke is applying intense sports-nutrition marketing — performance claims, neon labels, athlete sponsorships — to flavors that would make anyone slower.

## 2. Grudge Granite

**Short description:** A memorial engraving company that carves the deceased's unresolved grudges into their headstones.

**Long description:** Grudge Granite is a family-owned monument company that lets clients pre-register their complaints — the neighbor who never returned the ladder, the brother-in-law who "knows what he did" — to be permanently engraved on their tombstone. Premium packages include footnotes, a second-side "continued..." panel, and an annual grave-side reading service so the grudge is never forgotten.

**Themes and joke:** Extra dark humor (theme 3) with a ridiculous subscription add-on (theme 7). The joke is treating pettiness as a sacred legacy worth literally setting in stone, complete with a recurring maintenance plan.

## 3. Sure Roll Dice Co.

**Short description:** An artisanal dice maker whose handcrafted dice have the same number on every face.

**Long description:** Sure Roll Dice Co. sells premium hardwood and resin dice "for people who hate uncertainty." Every die is hand-polished, individually numbered, and guaranteed to roll the number printed on all six sides. The catalog includes the Confidence Six, the Humble One, and a $200 collector's set of all six values "so you're prepared for any outcome you already chose." Returns are not accepted because "you knew exactly what you were getting."

**Themes and joke:** Useless product (theme 5) with ultra-luxury craftsmanship marketing (theme 4). The joke is lavishing artisanal care and premium pricing on a product that removes the entire point of dice.

## 4. Second Wind Air Cellars

**Short description:** A subscription service that ships members a monthly jar of artisanal air from exotic locations.

**Long description:** Second Wind Air Cellars is a "boutique atmospheric sommelier house" that bottles air from destinations like a Swiss alpine meadow, a Portland coffee shop at 7 a.m., and the parking lot of a mid-tier casino. Members receive a jar each month with tasting notes ("bold notes of pine, a lingering finish of regret") and access to the Cellar Club, where vintage jars — including the coveted 2019 Pre-Everything Reserve — trade at auction. An add-on freshness plan sends a technician to re-tighten your lids quarterly.

**Themes and joke:** Ridiculous subscription (theme 7) meets ultra-luxury where no market should exist (theme 4). The joke is wine-snob culture applied to literal nothing, plus a maintenance plan for jars that need no maintenance.

## 5. Hank & Sons Reactor Barn

**Short description:** A folksy, family-owned small-town shop that designs and builds nuclear reactors.

**Long description:** Operating out of a converted feed store in rural Nebraska, Hank & Sons Reactor Barn has been "keepin' the neutrons flowin' since 1987." Hank Sr. handles the thermal hydraulics, Hank Jr. runs the front counter, and cousin Denise does containment domes on weekends. The shop offers free coffee, a punch card (tenth reactor is half off), and a hand-painted sign reading "If we can't fission it, it can't be fissioned." They do not have a website; you have to call, and Hank answers.

**Themes and joke:** Enormous industrial product played as a mom-and-pop business (theme 9), with a wink at dubious legality (theme 6). The joke is the collision of homespun small-town charm with the most heavily regulated, high-stakes engineering on Earth.

## 6. Murky Springs Soda Co.

**Short description:** A craft soda company whose small-batch flavors are all liquids nobody ever wanted to drink.

**Long description:** Murky Springs Soda Co. is a Pacific Northwest craft soda house offering carbonated takes on Hot Dog Water, Public Pool, Aquarium Gravel, and their best-seller, New Tire Smell. Each bottle is cold-filtered, cane-sugar sweetened, and comes with pairing suggestions ("New Tire pairs beautifully with a long wait at the DMV"). Their seasonal release, Rain Gutter Reserve, sells out every fall to people who insist they're buying it ironically.

**Themes and joke:** Gross flavors (theme 1). The joke is the full craft-beverage treatment — small batches, tasting notes, seasonal drops — applied to flavors that are technically beverages only in the legal sense.

## 7. Waxing Nostalgic

**Short description:** An artisanal candle company that makes candles from donated human earwax.

**Long description:** Waxing Nostalgic operates donation centers in twelve states where "wax benefactors" contribute their earwax in exchange for store credit. The wax is pooled, filtered, and hand-poured into scented candles with names like Sunday at Grandma's, Locker Room Memories, and Q-Tip Confessional. The company insists the product is "the most personal ambiance on the market" and offers a premium single-origin line sourced from one very committed donor named Gary.

**Themes and joke:** Simply gross (theme 2) with artisanal luxury branding (theme 4). The joke is boutique candle-shop pretension built on a supply chain nobody should ever think about, plus the words "single-origin" doing horrifying work.

## 8. Ashes to Ashtrays

**Short description:** A memorial studio that turns your loved one's cremated remains into a decorative ashtray.

**Long description:** Ashes to Ashtrays is a "legacy ceramics studio" that kiln-fires cremains into handsome ashtrays, so the departed can "keep holding your ashes, like they always said they would." Options include the Poetic Justice model for relatives who warned you about smoking, a matching coaster set for the whole estate, and an heirloom program that adds each new generation to the same growing ashtray. The showroom has a wall of testimonials no one has ever read aloud without pausing.

**Themes and joke:** Extra dark humor (theme 3). The joke is the funeral industry's tasteful, soft-spoken branding wrapped around an idea that is one long circular pun about mortality.

## 9. Threshold Atelier

**Short description:** A luxury house selling hand-woven cashmere doormats starting at $40,000.

**Long description:** Threshold Atelier of Geneva produces doormats woven from baby cashmere, mulberry silk, and thread-of-gold, each requiring nine months of loom work. The flagship Sovereign Welcome mat is monogrammed, insured as fine art, and explicitly designed to be wiped with the filth of shoes. Clients are assigned a personal Entryway Consultant, and the brochure notes that "true wealth is watching guests hesitate." A trade-in program accepts lightly soiled mats for 10% off, which are then destroyed in a private ceremony.

**Themes and joke:** Ultra luxury where no market should exist (theme 4). The joke is maximal craftsmanship and wealth-signaling poured into an object whose entire purpose is to be stepped on.

## 10. Blank Maps Cartography Co.

**Short description:** A heritage map maker whose maps are completely blank.

**Long description:** Blank Maps Cartography Co. sells museum-grade, acid-free, hand-deckled maps with nothing on them, "for the explorer who refuses to be told where things are." The catalog includes the Voyager (blank, cream), the Navigator (blank, ivory), and the limited Antarctic Edition (blank, but colder somehow). Each map ships in a leather tube with a certificate of inauthenticity. The FAQ's only entry is "No."

**Themes and joke:** Useless product (theme 5) sold with heirloom-craft seriousness (theme 4). The joke is premium cartography branding on a product that is, by every measurable standard, paper.

## 11. HOV Buddy

**Short description:** A rental service supplying realistic mannequin passengers for carpool-lane commuters.

**Long description:** HOV Buddy rents hyper-realistic passengers by the week so solo drivers can "carpool with confidence." The lineup includes Nodding Greg, Sleeping Aunt Carol, and the deluxe Conversational Dave, whose head turns at stoplights. Plans include seasonal outfit swaps, a "traffic stop protection plan" that the terms of service admit protects nothing, and an emergency hotline that only plays hold music. The website's legal page is a single shrug emoticon.

**Themes and joke:** Dubious legality (theme 6) with a ridiculous subscription layer (theme 7). The joke is a company building tiered pricing, accessories, and customer support around what is straightforwardly a traffic violation.

## 12. Left Behind Hosiery

**Short description:** A subscription that mails you exactly one sock per month — always the left one.

**Long description:** Left Behind Hosiery ships members a single, high-quality left sock every month, on the theory that "the dryer is going to take one anyway — get ahead of it." Tiers include Solo (one left sock), Widower (one left sock plus a condolence card for the right), and Executive (one left sock, expedited). The company's pair-completion program lets you buy the matching right sock for triple the price, but it is perpetually out of stock. Cancellation requires mailing back one sock, which restarts your subscription.

**Themes and joke:** Ridiculous subscription (theme 7) meets useless product (theme 5). The joke is a recurring-revenue business model engineered around a household annoyance, deliberately making the problem it claims to solve.

## 13. Blinkr

**Short description:** A venture-backed startup whose app reminds you to blink.

**Long description:** Blinkr is a Series B "ocular wellness platform" that sends push notifications telling you to blink, something you were already doing. The founders describe blinking as "a legacy manual process ripe for disruption" and tout their proprietary BlinkScore, streak tracking, and a premium tier that reminds you slightly more often. The pitch deck claims a total addressable market of "everyone with eyes," and investors have valued the company at $1.4 billion. The app's only setting is off, which is Pro-only.

**Themes and joke:** Incredibly mundane idea played as a hot tech startup (theme 8). The joke is the full startup ceremony — metrics, paywalls, disruption language — around an involuntary reflex.

## 14. Marge's Orbital Launch & Diner

**Short description:** A roadside Kansas diner that also provides commercial satellite launch services.

**Long description:** Marge's Orbital Launch & Diner has been serving pie and low-Earth-orbit insertions off Route 56 since 1994. Marge handles trajectory calculations between refilling coffees, her husband Bill runs the launch pad out back behind the grain silo, and Tuesday's special is meatloaf with a free payload inspection. The menu laminate includes a delta-v chart, launches are scheduled "weather and church permitting," and regulars know to ask for booth six, which rattles the least during liftoff.

**Themes and joke:** Aerospace-scale operation played as a small-town mom-and-pop (theme 9). The joke is billion-dollar space-industry logistics run with diner hospitality, where the biggest scheduling constraint is Sunday service.

## 15. Adopt-A-Cone

**Short description:** A nonprofit that lets you sponsor an orphaned traffic cone with a monthly donation.

**Long description:** Adopt-A-Cone rescues traffic cones abandoned after construction projects and places them in "forever lanes." For $9 a month, sponsors receive a photo of their cone, quarterly updates on its condition ("Waverly has faded slightly but remains upright"), and a certificate of adoption. The Legacy Circle tier lets donors name a cone after a loved one, and the annual gala features a moving slideshow of cones lost to snowplows. The charity's overhead is 98%, which the newsletter calls "cone-forward spending."

**Themes and joke:** Ridiculous subscription (theme 7) wrapped in nonprofit charity tropes. The joke is animal-rescue emotional marketing — adoption photos, updates, memorial tributes — applied to public roadway plastic.
