The house

It began just above the glass

Marguerite Voss spent eleven years as a wine sommelier before admitting the truth to herself: the part she loved was the moment before the sip, when the air above the glass told you everything. The wine, frankly, kept getting in the way.

She sold her collection, kept the jars, and went to the mountains. Second Wind now maintains forty harvest sites on four continents, each worked by trained gatherers who seal at origin and carry the jars down by hand. The cellar in Sonoma holds six thousand jars at a constant temperature, for reasons our cellarmaster has asked us not to question.

"People ask what separates our air from the air in their home. Provenance. Also, their air is free, which should tell you something about it." Marguerite Voss, founder and nose emerita

Freshness assurance

Dale will be there Tuesday

Air does not go bad, but lids can loosen, and a loose lid is a philosophical emergency. Our technicians carry calibrated torque wrenches and speak in reassuring tones. Each visit ends with a signed certificate stating that your nothing remains intact.

The plan covers unlimited jars, because once you own forty of them, you will want a professional involved.

The Cellar Club

Where vintages change hands

Twice a year, Reserve members gather to bid on jars that no longer exist anywhere else. The 1998 Grand Hotel Lobby went for four figures last spring. The buyer described it as "an investment," and his family has learned not to follow up.

The crown of the cellar remains the 2019 Pre-Everything Reserve, harvested in an ordinary office in late 2019. Nose of birthday cake, handshakes, and unearned confidence. Eleven jars remain. We open none of them.
