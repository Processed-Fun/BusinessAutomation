Our Craft

From the Urn to the Mantel

You send us the urn. White gloves take it from there. A measured portion of the remains is folded into the glaze, the vessel is fired at 2,300 degrees, and what returns to you is a certified ceramic ashtray in a velvet case, with provenance papers and a card explaining the concept to guests.

Our ceramicists trained in Italy. Our grief counselor trained the ceramicists. Every gram of ash is weighed and logged, because your grandmother deserves an audit trail.

The Place of Honor

An Urn Waits. An Ashtray Participates.

An urn sits in a corner and asks to be dusted. An ashtray takes part in the evening. It hosts, it listens, and it gathers what you were going to flick somewhere anyway. Grief counselors call this a continuing bond. Our clients call it Tuesday.

Every vessel ships with a walnut mantel stand, felt feet, and a small brass placard bearing the departed's name and years, so guests understand before they stub.

A Gentle Process

You Grieve. We Fire.

A consultation begins with tea and ends with a glaze selection. We collect the urn by courier, in an unmarked vehicle if the family prefers, and most do. Firing takes three weeks. The waiting, our consultants say, is part of the ceremony, and also how kilns work.

Planning ahead? Our pre-need Patience Plan lets you reserve your own firing and pick your glaze while you can still be consulted. Your family will thank you, once they understand.
